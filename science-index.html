Science Intelligence™

When someone asks an AI about the science behind your category, something gets cited. Science Index™ makes sure your evidence is in the running.

What It Is

A set of evidence-led articles and FAQs about the science behind your product, written from your own verified research and structured so search engines and AI assistants can find, read and quote them. It is scientific content marketing, built on a real evidence file rather than on keywords.

The question moved

People used to search and click through ten results. Increasingly they ask one question and read one answer, assembled from whatever the system found most credible.

Somebody gets cited

That answer is built from published sources. In most health categories the cited material was not written by the brands who did the science.

You already have the evidence

If we have run Science Intelligence™, the studies are verified and the claims are scoped. The work left is publishing it where questions get asked.

01 · Start from the evidence file

We already did the reading.

Science Index™ runs off the same evidence workbook Science Intelligence™ produces: real studies, verified identifiers, load-bearing work read in full text. The articles are written from that file, which is why they hold up when somebody follows a citation.

What the articles are built on

  • Verified studies with resolving identifiers
  • Findings recorded in both directions
  • Claims already scoped to your regulatory class
  • Content written from a keyword list
02 · Answer the question, not the keyword

People ask assistants full questions now.

Nobody types “magnesium absorption bioavailability” into an assistant. They ask whether one form actually works better than another. Each piece is built around a real question, answered directly in the opening lines, with the evidence underneath for anyone who keeps reading.

The shape of a piece

The answer, immediately

The evidence

03 · Science first, product second

It works because it is not an ad.

Content that opens by selling gets treated as marketing by readers and by the systems that summarise the web. These pieces are about the science itself, which is also what keeps them inside what a regulated brand is allowed to publish. Your product appears as the brand that did the work, not as the answer to every question.

FAQ

Science Index™ — common questions

What is Science Index™?

A set of evidence-led articles and FAQs about the science behind your product, written from your verified research and structured so search engines and AI assistants can read and cite them. The subject is the science, not the product.

How is this different from SEO content?

Most content programmes start from a keyword list and work backwards to something to say. This starts from an evidence file: real studies, verified identifiers, findings recorded in both directions. That is what makes it citable rather than just optimised, and it is why it survives a regulated review process.

Why not just write about the product?

Two reasons. Content that reads as promotion gets weighted as promotion, by readers and by the systems summarising the web. And in a regulated category, product claims carry obligations that a piece about the underlying science does not. Writing about the mechanism keeps the content both credible and publishable.

Do we need Science Intelligence™ first?

It is the natural starting point, because the articles are written from that evidence workbook. If you already hold verified research of your own, we can work from that instead.

Who publishes it?

You do, on your own site, so the credibility accrues to your brand. We deliver the content ready for review along with an implementation guide, and we spend time with your web team getting the first set live.

How does this fit with the rest of the engine?

It sits under Science Intelligence™. Lit Review™ finds the evidence, Claim Dev™ turns it into claims, Science Story™ turns those into a narrative, and Science Index™ publishes the science underneath all of it where questions are being asked.
