What It Is

A fast, strategic review of the science surrounding a product, ingredient, condition, mechanism, category, competitors, and consumer misconceptions — built to find scientific white space, identify defensible claims, guide formulation, and uncover stronger positioning before creative begins.

Inside A Lit Review™

Five passes, before anything reaches a claim.

Step 01 · Scope

We start from the mechanism, not the category.

A category search returns everything your competitors already say. We scope to the biological mechanism and the benefit territory instead, because that is where the unclaimed ground is.

Search scope

MechanismBenefit territoryIngredient & formCompetitor claimsCategory boilerplateMarketing copy
Step 02 · Sourcing

Peer-reviewed literature and trial registries, searched directly.

Randomised controlled trials, systematic reviews and meta-analyses carry the most weight. We favour the last ten to fifteen years and reach further back where the work is foundational.

Weighting

Randomised controlled trialsHighest
Systematic reviews & meta-analysesHigh
Observational & cohortSupporting
In vitro & animalRecorded only
Step 03 · Screening

What gets through, and what does not.

Human studies. Relevant population. Dose and delivery relevant to the product. Preclinical work is recorded and tagged, and never carried into consumer-facing language.

Screening rules

  • Human subjects
  • Population matches who buys the product
  • Dose and form comparable to the formula
  • Peer-reviewed
  • In vitro and animal work — logged, never claimed on
Step 04 · Verification

Every identifier gets opened.

We confirm each identifier resolves to the study actually cited, and every number that reaches a claim is checked against its own source rather than a secondary citation. It is dull work. It is also where most claim files quietly fall apart.

Our full-text rule

A study is read in full text before a claim rests on it whenever it is pivotal to that claim, or it is the sole support for it.

Abstracts routinely lead with a subgroup result when the primary endpoint missed, drop confidence intervals, and stay loose on dose and duration. Roughly three to ten studies per project get the full read.

Step 05 · The record

Every study becomes a row you can audit.

Design, direction, the finding in plain language, and whether we read the abstract or the full text. Null and mixed results sit in the same file as everything else.

Every deliverable carries a method statement: how many studies screened, how many read in full.

FAQ

Lit Review™ — common questions

What is a Lit Review™?

A Lit Review™ is a fast, strategic review of the published science behind your product — mapping mechanisms, white space, misconceptions, and the claims you could credibly own. It's Phase 1 of the Science Marketing Engine.

How long does a Lit Review™ take?

Weeks, not the six months a traditional review can take. Our AI-assisted process compresses the timeline without cutting corners on rigor.

Who is a Lit Review™ for?

Consumer health and healthcare brands deciding where to invest, what to claim, or how to differentiate — ideally before committing to a formula or a campaign.

What do I get from a Lit Review™?

A clear map of the evidence: mechanisms, claim opportunities scored against the standard that matters (retailer or regulatory), competitive white space, and a prioritized direction for marketing.

How is it different from a standard literature review?

A standard review tells you what the studies say. A Lit Review™ tells you where your brand can credibly win — built for marketing decisions, not just academic completeness.

How is this different from an AI literature-search tool?

A search tool gives your team a faster way to find papers. It does not screen them, verify that identifiers resolve, read the load-bearing ones in full text, or decide what the evidence will and will not support. That work is the review, and it is what we deliver. We wrote a fuller comparison here: IngredientAI vs. Let There Be.
